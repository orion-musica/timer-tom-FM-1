Manual

Timer Tom 0.1 BETA for the M-VAVE FM-1. Built on FM1 Open V14.

Names used here. Buttons are written like PLAY. The seven encoders are SELECT, ALGORITHM, PRESETS and K1–K4 (the four knobs under the screen). The keyboard has 16 white and 11 black keys.

1. First start

After the start screen (an hourglass runs while the engine starts; any key skips it) the KITCHEN page opens with four empty timers. Nothing is loaded from your projects: Timer Tom sets up its own three sounds every time it starts.

2. The KITCHEN page

It is the only page and it never hides a timer. From the top:

  • Header: TIMER TOM, the loop sound, the mode (RHYTHM, TICK 1, TICK 2, SILENT), the running loop (L13) and the octave (when not 0) and a small hourglass of the timer that ends first.
  • Four rows, one per timer: the dish icon, the time left in big digits (MM:SS, or H:MM:SS from one hour), then K1 RUN (knob and state: OFF, SET, RUN, PAUSE, READY!), the dish name and the time that was set. A bar under the digits empties like sand. The active timer is shown inverted.
  • Three hint lines: which knobs do what, what PLAY will do now for the active timer, and the other buttons. Messages such as TIMER 2 RUNS appear inverted in the last line for a moment.

3. Setting a timer

Turn K1, K2, K3 or K4. The timer becomes active and its time changes. The step grows with the time: 5 seconds up to 1 minute, 15 seconds up to 5 minutes, 30 seconds up to 15 minutes, 1 minute up to an hour, then 5 minutes. Turning fast speeds it up. The longest time is 4 hours. Turning back to --:-- switches the timer off.

A timer that runs, is paused or is ringing is only made active by the first click of its knob, so a quick touch never changes it. The next clicks change the time left (a running timer keeps running).

4. Dish icons

PRESETS changes the icon of the active timer: EGGS, PASTA, RICE, POTATO, SOUP, MEAT, FISH, PIZZA, CAKE, BREAD, VEG, TEA. The timers start as eggs, pasta, rice and meat.

5. Start, pause, all at once

PLAY starts the active timer, or pauses it when it runs. Hold PLAY: a bar fills under the title and after 0.6 s every timer that is set or paused starts together. If none is waiting but some run, holding PLAY pauses all of them.

6. When a timer is ready

It becomes the active timer, its row blinks, TIMER 1 IS READY appears, the keys flash and a short kalimba motif sounds every 2.4 seconds. Each timer has its own motif, so you can hear which dish is done. The chime stops after three minutes; the blinking goes on.

Press PLAY or turn that timer's knob to stop it. The timer goes back to the time it was set to, ready for the next batch. Hold PLAY to stop every alarm at once.

7. Reset and clear

REC puts the active timer back to its set time (paused). Press REC again to clear it to --:--.

8. Key loops

Each of the 27 keys starts its own background loop: four bars, one long pad note per bar, each note held until the next. All loops stay in one gentle key (A minor / C major pentatonic, no half steps) and move mostly by step, so they never clash and do not pull your attention from the cooking. Another key switches the loop; the same key or HOME stops it. After four rounds (about 33 seconds) the loop moves on to the next key by itself, and on and on; the screen says NEXT LOOP. The key of the running loop blinks. SELECT chooses the sound: GLASS PAD, STRINGS, WARM PAD or CHOIR, all with a slow swell, a slow LFO on pitch and volume, a long tail and plenty of reverb. OCT − / OCT + transpose the loop by octaves.

9. Rhythm and tick

ALGORITHM chooses what you hear while timers run:

  • RHYTHM (the default): every running timer plays one element of a soft ambient-techno groove at 118 BPM with a little swing: timer 1 a long dark kick, timer 2 an open hat on the offbeats, timer 3 a rim with dub delay, timer 4 maracas in sixteenths. Start one timer and only its drum plays; start more and they join. A paused or ready timer drops out, so you also hear which one is done. The hourglass grain falls on every beat.
  • TICK 1 / TICK 2: a soft dry clock tick once a second instead, alternately tick and tock, a little clearer in the last ten seconds.
  • SILENT: no rhythm and no tick; loops and the alarm still play.

10. Lights

The keys show an hourglass for the timer that ends first (the active one, if it runs). The 11 black keys are the upper bulb: they go out from the edges towards the middle as time passes. The 16 white keys are the lower bulb: they fill from the middle outwards. On every beat (RHYTHM) or every second (TICK) a grain falls: the middle black key flashes, then a middle white key. The key of the running loop blinks twice a second. PLAY lights for half of every second while timers run and blinks fast while one is ready. REC is lit when the active timer can be reset.

11. Troubleshooting

No sound. Turn up MASTER. No tick. Turn ALGORITHM; the header shows the level. Stuck notes. HOME + PLAY stops every sound. Back to stock. Open the installer and choose Restore original V15.
